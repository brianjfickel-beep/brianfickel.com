About

Why I built this

For almost 20 years I have advised senior leaders on communication: as a speechwriter, spokesperson and communications advisor to commanders at the division, corps, combatant command and service-chief levels, in Washington and in war zones.

Nobody handed me a manual. I learned this work three ways. Executive education gave me the theory: graduate study in public relations and corporate communications at Georgetown, and in strategy at the U.S. Army War College. Self-development filled in the rest: reading widely, studying the advisors I admired, writing and rewriting, and asking leaders afterward what had actually helped them. And I learned plenty the hard way: advice that arrived a day late, a message that was right and still landed wrong, crises that taught more in a week than a semester could.

What stays with me is how much of it could have been taught. Developing people is the part of this work I love most, which is why I teach senior leader communication at the graduate level and why I built this site.

This is strictly a passion project. Everything here is free. I offer no services and earn nothing from it. It exists to pass on what I learned, so the next advisor can learn it faster than I did.

Focus

Advising is a discipline

Anyone can write a memo. Few people can sit beside a senior leader, read the room and the record, and tell them what they need to hear in time for it to matter. That work can be learned, and it should be taught.
